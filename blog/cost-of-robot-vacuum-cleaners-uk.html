Cost of Robot Vacuum Cleaners UK

13 November 2026

The cost of robot vacuum cleaners in the UK runs from around £100 for a simple model to well over £1,000 for a flagship with a self-cleaning dock. Knowing what you get at each price, and what spares cost once you own one, helps you choose a robot that fits both your budget and your home.

Quick answer: On the makers’ own UK online stores in November 2026, robot vacuum prices ran from £99.99 (Roborock Q7 M5, on sale) to £1,599 (eufy Omni S2). Many well-equipped models with all-in-one docks sat between about £320 and £650. Running costs are mainly replacement filters, brushes and dust bags.

Cost of robot vacuum cleaners in the UK in 2026: full breakdown

The cost of a robot vacuum comes down to its features, its dock and the brand. At the bottom of the range, Roborock’s Q7 M5 was £99.99 and its Q10 S5 £109.99 on Roborock’s UK store in November 2026 (both sale prices). Both list LiDAR navigation and anti-tangle brushes, which a few years ago were only found on dearer models.

In the middle, the Roborock Qrevo S Pro (£319.99 on sale) and the eufy Omni C20 (£499) add docks that look after the robot for you, such as emptying the dustbin and washing the mop, while the Roborock Qrevo Edge 2 (£449.99 on sale) lists 25,000 Pa suction and AI obstacle recognition. For families with pets or larger homes, this is often where the best value sits.

At the top, the eufy Omni C30 (£649), Roborock Saros 20 (£799 for the Saros 20 Set on sale), eufy Omni E35 (£999) and eufy Omni S2 (£1,599) add higher suction figures, mop-washing docks and AI obstacle recognition or navigation. These suit larger homes or anyone who wants the most hands-off cleaning.

What drives the cost of robot vacuum cleaners in the UK?

The cost of a robot vacuum is closely linked to its features. Cheaper models focus on vacuuming and simple mapping. As you move up the price bands, you’ll find room-by-room cleaning, no-go zones, higher suction ratings, self-emptying dustbins, mop washing and AI obstacle recognition.

Brand matters too. Well-known names usually have better UK support and easier-to-find spares, while newer brands may offer similar features for less. Read recent UK reviews before you decide.

Don’t forget to factor in the cost of accessories and spares. Some brands bundle extra filters and brushes, while others require separate purchases. Check if your chosen robot has affordable, easily available consumables in the United Kingdom before committing.

Robot vacuum specs and your rights in the UK

Suction (in pascals), battery run-time and dustbin size are the makers’ own figures, and brands don’t all measure them the same way, so compare like with like. Whoever you buy from, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 says goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described. Citizens Advice can help if something goes wrong.

Robot Vacuum Maintenance Costs and Longevity

Once you own a robot, the running costs are replacement parts. On Roborock’s UK store in November 2026, a three-pack of dust bags was £19.99, two washable filters for the Qrevo range £29.99 and a main brush £15.99 (sale). eufy sold accessory kits for its C30 and E35 robots at £59.99 each. How often you need them depends on how often the robot runs. For more on this, read our article on top robot cleaners in London.

Self-emptying docks save you a job but use dust bags, and mopping docks need cleaning solution and mop cloths. Check what comes in the box and whether spares are easy to buy for your model.

Regular maintenance, such as emptying the bin, cleaning sensors and untangling hair from brushes, keeps your robot running well and helps it last. Our robot vacuum maintenance guide explains each step.

Robot Vacuum Troubleshooting: Common Issues

Even good robot vacuums can get stuck, miss areas or need frequent bin emptying. Reading UK reviews can help you choose a model known for reliability, and it is worth checking that spare parts and support are easy to get.

Many common problems are solved with a software update or by keeping floors clear of loose cables and small objects. Most big brands update their apps and robots regularly.

Robot vacuum energy use and running costs

Robot vacuums run on a rechargeable battery and top up on their dock, so they add little to a household electricity bill. To work out your own figure, check the charging power in the manual and multiply by your electricity unit rate and the hours it spends charging.

Many models have a quieter, lower-power mode for light cleans, and mapping means the robot doesn’t waste time going over the same patch twice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do robot vacuums cost in the UK in 2026?

On the makers’ own UK stores in November 2026, prices ran from £99.99 (Roborock Q7 M5, on sale) to £1,599 (eufy Omni S2). Many models with all-in-one docks cost between about £320 and £650.

What features should I prioritise in a robot vacuum?

Focus on mapping, suction power, battery life and app control. If you have pets or allergies, look for anti-tangle brushes and a good filter. For busy households, a self-emptying dock and reliable navigation are worth the extra cost.

What are the yearly maintenance costs?

The main costs are replacement dust bags, filters, brushes and, on mopping models, mop cloths. As a guide, Roborock’s UK store sold three dust bags for £19.99 and two washable filters for £29.99 in November 2026.

Are robot vacuums energy efficient for UK homes?

Yes, they use little electricity because they run on a battery that recharges on the dock. Check the charging power in the manual if you want to work out your own running cost.

Where can I find reliable robot vacuum reviews in the UK?

Look for UK-based consumer sites, reputable magazines, and trusted retailers. Reviews from UK homeowners are more likely to reflect UK housing types and floor plans.

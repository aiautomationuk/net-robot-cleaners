Cleaning After Building Work

23 November 2026

Cleaning after building work goes fastest if you do it in the right order: clear the debris, then work from the top of each room down, lift the dust with a vacuum and damp cloths instead of sweeping it into the air, and leave the floors until last. Expect to go round twice, because fine dust keeps settling after the builders have gone.

Quick answer: To clean after building work, remove rubble and packaging first, open the windows, then clean each room from the ceiling down using a vacuum and damp microfibre cloths. Do the floors last, repeat a day or two later, and keep your robot vacuum out of the way until the heavy dust has gone.

Key Takeaways

  • Work top to bottom. Dust falls, so ceilings and walls come before surfaces and floors.

  • Lift dust, do not spread it. Vacuum and damp-wipe instead of dry sweeping or dusting.

  • Plan to clean twice. Fine dust carries on settling after the work stops.

  • Protect yourself. Ventilate the room and wear a dust mask and gloves.

  • Save the robot vacuum for later. Heavy builders’ dust can clog and damage it.

Why Cleaning After Building Work Is Different

Ordinary household dust is light and sits on surfaces. Builders’ dust is a mixture of plaster, cement, brick, timber and filler, some of it gritty and some of it very fine. The fine part hangs in the air, travels through the house and settles on everything, including rooms the builders never went into.

It is also worth treating with some care. The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) says that regularly breathing construction dust can cause diseases such as lung cancer, asthma, COPD and silicosis. That warning is aimed at people who work with dust every day, but it is a good reason to clean in a way that keeps dust out of the air.

One important exception: if your home was built or refurbished before 2000 and you think the work may have disturbed asbestos, do not clean it up yourself. Stop, keep people out of the area and get professional advice.

Before You Start: Kit and Safety

  • A vacuum cleaner with a good filter, ideally a HEPA filter, plus spare bags or an empty bin.

  • Microfibre cloths and a bucket. Damp cloths trap dust; dry dusters move it around.

  • A dust mask, gloves and eye protection for the first, dustiest stage.

  • Strong rubble sacks for debris, offcuts and packaging.

  • A soft brush and dustpan for grit and larger pieces only.

Open the windows before you begin and keep internal doors shut on rooms you have already cleaned. If your builder is still to be chosen, our guide on how to find a reliable builder covers what to agree in advance, including who clears up.

Cleaning After Building Work, Step by Step

1. Clear the debris

Pick up rubble, offcuts, fixings and packaging by hand or with a dustpan and bag it. Check for nails and screws before you bring the vacuum out, as they can damage it.

2. Start at the top

Vacuum or damp-wipe ceilings, coving and light fittings with the power off. Then do the walls, working downwards. Freshly painted or plastered walls need a light touch, so use a barely damp cloth or a soft brush attachment and ask your decorator if you are unsure.

3. Windows, sills and woodwork

Vacuum window tracks and sills before you wipe them, otherwise the grit scratches. Then wipe frames, doors, skirting boards and radiators with a damp cloth, rinsing it often. Remember the tops of doors and door frames, which collect a surprising amount.

4. Fitted units and surfaces

Open every cupboard and drawer. Dust gets inside even when they were closed. Vacuum them out, wipe them down and leave them open to dry. Wipe worktops, shelves, sockets and switches last in this stage.

5. Floors last

Vacuum slowly, including the edges and under units, then mop hard floors with clean water, changing it often. A cloudy film that returns as the floor dries is fine dust, and it usually takes two or three passes to shift. Carpets and upholstery in nearby rooms will have taken in dust too, and our guide on how to clean upholstery at home explains how to deal with soft furnishings.

6. Go round again

Leave it a day or two, then repeat the wipe-down and vacuum. The second clean is much quicker and is the one that makes the house feel finished.

When Can You Use a Robot Vacuum Again?

A robot vacuum is built for everyday dust, crumbs and pet hair, not for building debris. Fine plaster and cement dust fills the bin and blocks the filter quickly, and grit can wear the brushes and wheels. Keep the robot shut away while the work is going on and during the first clean. Once the heavy cleaning is done, it earns its keep. Run it daily for the first week or two to collect the dust that keeps settling, empty the bin after every run and wash or replace the filter as the maker recommends. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does dust keep settling after building work?

Fine dust can stay in the air and keep settling for some time after the work stops, which is why one clean is rarely enough. Plan a first clean, then go round again a day or two later to pick up what has come down since.

Can I use my robot vacuum to clean up builders’ dust?

Not for the first clean. Fine plaster and cement dust can clog the filter and get into the motor and sensors, and grit can damage the brushes. Do the heavy clean by hand first, then let the robot take over for the light dust that settles afterwards, emptying the bin and cleaning the filter more often than usual.

Is builders’ dust harmful?

The Health and Safety Executive warns that regularly breathing construction dust can cause serious lung diseases, which is mainly a risk for people who work with it. For a one-off clean at home, the sensible approach is to avoid putting dust back into the air: wipe with damp cloths, vacuum instead of dry sweeping, ventilate the room and wear a dust mask.

Should the builder clean up when the job is finished?

It depends on what you agreed. Many builders leave a site swept and free of rubble, which is often called a builder’s clean, but that is not the same as a home that is ready to live in. Check what your quote or contract says before the work starts.

What should I clean first after building work?

Start by removing rubble and packaging, then work from the top of each room downwards: ceilings and light fittings, walls, windows and sills, surfaces, and floors last. Cleaning the floor first only means doing it twice.

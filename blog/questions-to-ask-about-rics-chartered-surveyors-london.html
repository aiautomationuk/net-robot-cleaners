Questions to Ask About RICS Chartered Surveyors London

21 November 2026

Questions to ask about RICS chartered surveyors London buyers, sellers and renovators should have ready cover qualifications, the type of survey, fees and what the report will tell you. Asking them helps you choose a qualified surveyor, understand the survey process and avoid surprises. Whether you’re a first-time buyer or an experienced homeowner, knowing what to ask puts you in control of your London property purchase.

Quick answer: The most important questions to ask a RICS chartered surveyor in London cover their RICS membership, experience, the survey levels they offer, fees, how they report and how they handle complaints. The answers help you choose the right professional, understand the cost and get a reliable survey of your London home.

Key Takeaways

  • Always check a surveyor’s RICS membership before instructing them in London.

  • Key questions cover qualifications, survey levels, costs, reporting and turnaround times.

  • RICS surveyors follow the RICS Home Survey Standard, which gives buyers and owners a consistent level of service.

  • Survey fees in London depend on the property’s size, age and value and the level of survey, so ask for a written quote.

  • A survey is not a legal requirement, but a mortgage valuation is not a substitute for one.

Why Ask the Right Questions About RICS Chartered Surveyors in London?

Importance of Choosing an Accredited Surveyor

Instructing a RICS chartered surveyor in London means you receive a professional service held to ethical and technical standards. The Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS) is the professional body that regulates chartered surveyors, and its members must keep their knowledge up to date and follow the RICS Home Survey Standard when surveying homes. A surveyor who is not regulated in this way may not work to the same standard.

Protecting Your Investment in the United Kingdom

Your property is likely to be your biggest asset. Asking the right questions protects that investment, because a thorough and impartial survey can reveal hidden issues, inform negotiations, or stop you making a costly mistake. London’s property market is competitive, which makes due diligence essential.

Understanding Surveyor Qualifications and Experience

RICS chartered surveyors must meet set educational and professional requirements and keep learning throughout their careers. When comparing surveyors, ask about their years of experience, their specialisms (such as historic buildings or flats) and their local knowledge. A surveyor who knows boroughs like Camden, Lambeth or Westminster will recognise the issues common there, such as movement in older terraces or leasehold concerns in flats.

Ensuring Compliance with RICS Standards

Compliance with RICS standards is essential. The Home Survey Standard sets out what each level of survey must cover and how it is reported. Ask surveyors how they keep their knowledge up to date and how they handle complaints. A reputable RICS member will be open about both, and about the professional indemnity insurance they hold.

Top Questions to Ask About RICS Chartered Surveyors London

What RICS accreditation do you hold?

Confirm your surveyor is a current member of RICS (MRICS or FRICS), which means they are professionally regulated. Ask for their name and firm, then check them on the RICS Find a Surveyor service for peace of mind.

What types of property surveys do you offer in London?

Different survey levels suit different properties. Ask whether they offer RICS Home Survey Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3, and which is best for your property. For example, a Level 3 survey (formerly the Building Survey) suits older or extensively altered homes, while a Level 2 survey (formerly the HomeBuyer Report) may be enough for a conventional flat or house in reasonable condition.

Can I see a sample survey report?

Requesting a sample report helps you understand the level of detail and clarity to expect. Look for comprehensive commentary on structure, damp, insulation, electrics, and any recommendations for further investigation. A quality report should use plain English and include photographs for clarity.

How do you determine the cost of RICS surveys in London?

Ask for a clear breakdown of fees, including what the survey covers and any possible extras, such as specialist investigations. Costs in London vary with the property’s size, age and value and the level of survey you choose, so compare quotes from two or three firms. Always get a written quote.

What is your experience with London property types?

London’s housing stock is diverse, from Georgian terraces to post-war flats. Ask about previous surveys conducted in your London borough or property type. Knowledge of typical local issues—like subsidence in certain postcodes or flat roof problems in 1960s blocks—can be invaluable.

What survey methods do you use?

Modern RICS surveyors use a range of techniques, from traditional visual inspections to moisture meters, thermal imaging, and occasionally drones for roof checks. Ask which methods they plan to use for your property and how these go beyond the minimum standard.

How quickly will I receive my survey report?

Turnaround times vary with the surveyor’s workload and the complexity of the report. Ask for a specific timeframe, and whether the surveyor will talk you through the findings.

What happens if the survey finds significant problems?

Clarify the next steps if major defects or regulatory issues are uncovered. Will the surveyor recommend further specialist reports? Can they provide repair cost estimates? Understanding this in advance can help you plan negotiations or remedial works.

How do you keep up to date with RICS regulations and compliance?

RICS members must complete and record Continuing Professional Development (CPD) every year. Ask how your surveyor stays informed about changes to standards and regulation. A clear answer shows professionalism and builds trust.

Comparing RICS Survey Types, Costs and What Drives Them

Property Survey Types in the UK

Under the RICS Home Survey Standard there are three levels of survey: Level 1 (formerly the Condition Report), Level 2 (formerly the HomeBuyer Report) and Level 3 (formerly the Building Survey). Each offers a different level of detail. Level 1 describes the condition of the property and flags urgent defects, Level 2 adds more on repairs and maintenance, and Level 3 gives the most detailed assessment, which matters for older or unconventional homes.

How Costs Vary Across London

Survey costs in London tend to reflect higher property values, complexity and demand. Property size, age and ease of access also affect the price, so quotes for the same level of survey can differ between firms.

What Is Included in RICS Survey Fees?

Ask for an exact list of what’s covered: inspection, report, photographs, recommendations, and VAT. Some surveyors include aftercare, such as follow-up calls or clarification sessions. Any exclusions or potential extra costs (e.g., for drone surveys or damp testing) should be detailed upfront.

Can You Negotiate Survey Fees?

Some surveyors may offer a lower fee for straightforward properties, but most rates reflect the expertise and time involved. It is reasonable to ask, especially if you are instructing more than one survey.

Understanding RICS Regulations, Compliance and Quality Standards

What Are the Key RICS Standards for Property Surveys?

RICS sets standards for residential surveys through the Home Survey Standard, alongside its Rules of Conduct. These require clear terms of engagement, clear reporting and continuing professional development. RICS-regulated firms must also hold professional indemnity insurance, which protects clients if errors or omissions occur.

How Does RICS Membership Benefit Clients?

RICS membership means your surveyor is accountable to a recognised professional body. RICS-regulated firms must have a complaints-handling procedure that includes access to an independent redress scheme, so you have somewhere to go if you are unhappy with the service.

What Is the Difference Between RICS and Non-RICS Surveyors?

RICS chartered surveyors are subject to RICS regulation and its Rules of Conduct. Surveyors who are not RICS members are not covered by that regulation, although some belong to other bodies, such as the Residential Property Surveyors Association. Whoever you choose, check who regulates them and what insurance they hold.

How Are Surveyors Assessed for Quality and Compliance?

RICS regulates its members and firms and can investigate complaints. Members must record their CPD every year. Ask your surveyor about their recent professional development to confirm their commitment to good practice.

Common Issues Found in London Property Surveys

Structural Problems in London Homes

Common findings in London surveys include damp, subsidence, roof leaks, timber decay, and outdated electrics. Many properties—especially Victorian and Edwardian terraces—may hide costly defects only a thorough RICS survey will reveal. These issues can affect mortgage approvals and insurance premiums.

Damp, Mould and Insulation Concerns

London’s climate and older housing stock make damp and black mould frequent discoveries. Poor insulation and ventilation can also be flagged, impacting energy efficiency and living comfort. For tips on dealing with mould, see Robot Cleaners’ guide to black mould on walls.

Legal and Leasehold Risks

Surveyors often flag matters for your legal adviser to check, such as alterations that may have needed permission, or leasehold issues in flats. These can affect a property’s value and how easy it is to sell later. Ask your surveyor to highlight anything your solicitor or conveyancer should follow up.

What to Expect After a Survey

After your survey, expect a detailed report, follow-up explanation if needed, and recommendations for further action. Use the findings to renegotiate price, request repairs, or plan maintenance. For more moving tips, see our advice on the best time of year to move house UK.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I verify a surveyor’s RICS status?

Use the RICS Find a Surveyor service to confirm your surveyor’s firm is regulated by RICS, and look for the MRICS or FRICS designation after their name.

Is a RICS survey mandatory when buying in London?

No. A survey is not a legal requirement. Your lender will arrange a mortgage valuation, but that is for the lender’s benefit and is not a survey, so most buyers are advised to commission their own.

Can I use the same surveyor for multiple properties?

Yes. Many RICS surveyors work across London and can survey more than one property for you. Ask about their experience of each property’s area and type so the advice is relevant.

When should I book my survey during the buying process?

Book as soon as your offer is accepted, especially in busy London markets. Early booking secures your preferred date and allows time to address any issues before exchange. For advice on moving logistics, see how to keep a packing area clean.

What should I do if the survey finds major defects?

If significant defects arise, discuss them with your solicitor and consider renegotiating the price or requesting repairs. Your RICS surveyor should explain the implications and recommend next steps, including referrals to other specialists if needed.

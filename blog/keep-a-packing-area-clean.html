Keep a Packing Area Clean

11 November 2026

The quickest way to keep a packing area clean is to give everything a fixed place, get boxes and wrapping off the floor, empty waste before it builds up, and finish each shift with a five-minute clean-down. Whether you pack orders from a spare room, a garage or a warehouse unit, the same habits apply.

Quick answer: Set up one bench with tools and materials within reach, store stock and boxes on shelves rather than the floor, and keep bins for cardboard, plastic and general waste at the end of the bench. Wipe down and sweep at the end of every shift, and do a deeper clean weekly.

Key Takeaways

  • Give every tool and material a fixed place at the bench.

  • Keep boxes, wrap and stock off the floor to cut trip hazards.

  • Separate bins for cardboard, plastic and general waste make clearing quick.

  • A short clean-down at the end of every shift beats a big clean once a month.

  • Workplace law expects workplaces to be kept sufficiently clean.

Why a Clean Packing Area Matters

Packing creates mess quickly: cardboard offcuts, tape ends, labels, void fill and dust from boxes. Left alone, it spreads across the floor and onto the products you are sending. That slows packing down, causes picking and labelling mistakes, and creates slip and trip hazards.

For employers, it is also a legal duty. The Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992 say workplaces, and the furniture and fittings in them, must be kept sufficiently clean, and that waste must not be allowed to build up except in suitable containers. The Health and Safety Executive publishes free guidance on preventing slips and trips at work.

Step 1: Set Out the Bench

A clean packing area starts with a good layout. Work in a line: products arrive at one end, are packed in the middle and leave sealed and labelled at the other. Keep the things you use on every order within arm's reach, and everything else on shelves or in drawers.

  • Tape guns, scissors and knives on hooks or in a tray, not loose on the bench.

  • Boxes and mailers on shelves under or above the bench, sorted by size.

  • A roll holder for paper or bubble wrap so it doesn't unroll across the floor.

  • The label printer at the outgoing end, with its cable tidied away.

Older tables often become cluttered because there is nowhere else to put things. Benches with built-in shelving, roll holders and tool rails give each item a home. If your tables are past saving, a UK manufacturer such as Benchworks makes packing benches built around your products and the space you have.

Step 2: Keep the Floor Clear

Most mess in a packing area ends up on the floor, and that is where the risk is. Keep incoming stock on racking or pallets, not stacked in walkways. Flatten empty boxes straight away and put them in a cage or a dedicated bin. Run cables overhead or along walls rather than across the floor.

Step 3: Manage Waste as You Go

Put separate bins at the end of the bench for cardboard, soft plastics and general waste, so offcuts go straight in rather than onto the floor. Empty them before they overflow, and agree a collection with your waste carrier that matches how much you produce. Businesses have a duty of care for their waste, so use a registered carrier and keep the paperwork.

Step 4: Build a Daily and Weekly Routine

Every shift

  • Clear the bench and put tools back in their place.

  • Empty or compact the waste bins.

  • Wipe the bench surface and sweep or vacuum the floor.

  • Restock boxes and tape for the next shift.

Every week

  • Clean shelves, under the bench and behind racking.

  • Check tape guns, cutters and the label printer.

  • Look for damaged boxes, worn mats or loose cables.

Cardboard dust settles everywhere, including on light fittings, window sills and the tops of racking. A periodic deeper clean deals with the places a daily sweep misses, and our guide to what a deep clean includes sets out what to ask for if you bring someone in.

Step 5: Choose Surfaces That Are Easy to Clean

The right furniture makes it far easier to keep a packing area clean. Smooth, sealed worktops wipe down in seconds, while worn chipboard soaks up spills and sheds crumbs. Benches with a clear space underneath, or shelving raised off the floor, let you sweep or vacuum right through instead of working around stacked boxes. Rubber anti-fatigue mats help comfort but trap dust, so lift and clean beneath them weekly. Packing From Home

If you pack orders in a spare room or garage, the same rules help keep the rest of the house clean. Keep packing materials in one zone, close the door on the mess, and vacuum the area often, because cardboard fibres and polystyrene beads travel. Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a packing area be cleaned?

A quick clean-down at the end of every shift, with a deeper clean of shelves, racking and hard-to-reach places every week, works for most packing areas.

What is the biggest hazard in a packing area?

Clutter on the floor, such as boxes, wrap and cables, is a common cause of slips and trips. Keep walkways clear and store materials on shelves or racking.

Do I need separate bins for packing waste?

It helps. Separate bins for cardboard, plastic and general waste stop offcuts reaching the floor and make recycling collections easier.

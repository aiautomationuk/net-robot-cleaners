Hardwood Flooring vs Laminate UK Costs: 2026 Price Guide

11 September 2026

If you’re planning to update your home’s floors, the choice between hardwood and laminate can be daunting—especially when it comes to cost. With prices rising in 2026 and so many flooring types to compare, understanding hardwood flooring vs laminate UK costs is crucial for British homeowners wanting style, durability and value without overspending. Here’s your comprehensive, impartial guide to making the best decision for your budget and lifestyle.

Quick answer: In the UK, laminate flooring costs £10–£30 per m² (plus £10–£20 per m² for fitting), while hardwood flooring ranges from £40–£120 per m² (plus £20–£50 per m² for installation). Laminate is typically cheaper, but hardwood offers longer lifespan and added value for UK homes.

Key Takeaways

  • Laminate flooring remains the affordable leader in the United Kingdom, with total fitted costs from £20–£50 per m².
  • Hardwood flooring vs laminate UK costs: hardwood is pricier up-front (£60–£170 per m² fitted), but lasts decades if maintained.
  • Engineered wood offers a balance of cost, durability and style for British homes—often £50–£110 per m² fitted.
  • Maintenance, room type, and expected lifespan are key when choosing the right floor on a UK flooring budget.
  • Laminate is quick to install and easy to clean, while hardwood adds value and prestige but requires more care.

Hardwood Flooring vs Laminate UK Costs: Full 2026 Breakdown

When comparing hardwood flooring vs laminate UK costs in 2026, it’s clear that laminate is the budget-friendly choice for most British homes. For supply only, expect laminate to cost £10–£30 per m², while hardwood (solid or engineered) ranges from £40–£120 per m². Installation adds £10–£20 per m² for laminate, and £20–£50 per m² for hardwood, reflecting the extra skill and time required for real wood.

These figures can vary based on region, brand, and room complexity. For a typical 20m² UK living room, fitted costs are £400–£1,000 for laminate, £1,200–£3,400 for solid hardwood, and £1,000–£2,200 for engineered wood. Always check with reputable hardwood flooring retailers UK and get multiple quotes before committing.

What Drives Flooring Costs in the UK?

The biggest factors in hardwood flooring vs laminate UK costs are raw material quality, brand, and installation difficulty. Laminate is typically mass-produced, so it’s much cheaper than the processed timber and skilled labour required for hardwood. Complex layouts, stairs, or herringbone patterns drive up costs for both types. For those seeking affordable hardwood options UK, engineered boards and off-the-shelf finishes offer good value without sacrificing style.

Don’t forget underlay, trims, and disposal of old flooring—these extras can add £5–£20 per m². Buying from trusted UK retailers ensures compliance with British building standards and access to aftercare support.

Hardwood vs Laminate Flooring Pros and Cons

Both materials have unique advantages and drawbacks beyond cost. The hardwood vs laminate flooring pros and cons debate is about more than just price—it’s about aesthetics, longevity, upkeep, and suitability for different rooms in UK homes.

Hardwood flooring (both solid and engineered) is prized for natural beauty, warmth underfoot, and the ability to sand and refinish—making it a true long-lasting flooring option UK homeowners favour. With proper care, it can last 30–100 years and add significant value to your property. However, the hardwood flooring maintenance costs can be high: sanding, resealing, and cautious cleaning are needed, especially in the damp British climate.

Laminate flooring is popular for its durability, stain resistance, and simple upkeep. Modern laminate convincingly mimics wood, tile or stone, and comes in hundreds of styles. The main laminate flooring benefits UK buyers enjoy are its affordability, quick installation, and suitability for pets and children. However, it can’t be refinished—when badly damaged, it must be replaced. And while it’s water-resistant, it isn’t fully waterproof unless specifically rated.

Where Each Flooring Type Works Best

Hardwood is ideal for living rooms, bedrooms and hallways where you want a premium finish, but it’s less suitable for kitchens and bathrooms due to moisture. Engineered wood is better for these areas than solid wood, as it resists warping. Laminate is the all-rounder, working well in high-traffic spaces and rental properties where ease of cleaning is essential. For UK gardens rooms or conservatories, check suitability for fluctuating temperatures.

Installation and Maintenance: What UK Homeowners Need to Know

Fitting Costs and Process

Laminate’s click-fit and floating floor systems make it a strong DIY option, which can further cut hardwood flooring vs laminate UK costs for the hands-on homeowner. Professional fitting is still recommended for large or awkward rooms, costing £10–£20 per m². Solid hardwood, on the other hand, almost always requires skilled fitting—expect to pay £20–£50 per m² for proper subfloor prep, acclimatisation and expert installation.

Engineered wood sits in between, often using tongue-and-groove or click systems but still benefiting from professional fitting for best results. Regardless of type, a clean, level and dry subfloor is essential for all flooring—especially in older British properties where damp can be an issue.

Maintenance: Upkeep Costs Over Time

Hardwood’s ongoing costs are higher: regular sweeping, prompt spill clean-up, and every-decade sanding and sealing add up. Over 20 years, hardwood flooring maintenance costs can reach hundreds of pounds, especially if you hire professionals. Laminate is much easier—just sweep and mop (avoiding excess water), with most brands needing no special treatments. For busy UK households, this low-maintenance appeal makes laminate a top contender.

Budgeting for UK Homes: Hardwood vs Laminate in Real Terms

How Much Does Laminate Flooring Cost in the UK?

For most British homes, the cost question is key: how much does laminate flooring cost in the UK? In 2026, you’ll pay £10–£30 per m² for materials and £10–£20 per m² for fitting, making a 20m² room £400–£1,000 all-in. This is why laminate dominates the UK flooring market for renovations, rentals and first-time buyers.

The UK flooring budget guide suggests adding 10% for wastage, plus underlay (£2–£8 per m²) and trims. For those wanting a quick, affordable refresh without major disruption, laminate is hard to beat. Top brands like Quick-Step, Egger and Balterio offer realistic textures, long warranties, and easy DIY installation.

Solid, Engineered or Laminate: Which Lasts Longest?

Solid hardwood flooring can last 50–100 years with care, engineered wood 20–40 years, while laminate typically lasts 10–20 years depending on quality and traffic. Laminate’s shorter lifespan is offset by its lower price, but for period homes or forever homes, hardwood is often the preferred investment. Frequently Asked Questions

How do hardwood flooring vs laminate UK costs compare for large homes?

The price gap widens with size. A 50m² home fitted with laminate may cost £1,000–£2,500, while hardwood could be £3,000–£8,500 or more. Factor in maintenance over time—hardwood’s costs add up, but it can last for generations if looked after.

Is laminate flooring cheaper than hardwood for ongoing upkeep?

Yes, laminate is far cheaper to maintain. It only needs basic cleaning, while hardwood requires sanding, refinishing and special products. Over 20 years, hardwood maintenance could exceed £1,000, especially if you use professional services in the United Kingdom.

Can you install either type over underfloor heating in the UK?

Engineered wood and specialist laminate can work with underfloor heating, but solid hardwood is more sensitive to temperature changes. Always check manufacturer guidance and installer expertise before fitting flooring over heating systems.

What’s better for pets and kids: hardwood or laminate?

Laminate is more scratch- and stain-resistant, making it a popular choice for families with pets or young children. However, premium hardwood with a tough finish can also perform well—just expect more maintenance over time.

Does real wood flooring add more value to a UK home than laminate?

Generally, yes. Hardwood is seen as a premium feature and can boost resale value, especially in period or high-end properties. Laminate offers practicality and great looks, but usually won’t add as much value when selling your home.

Keeping Washing Fresh in Winter UK

9 November 2026

Keeping washing fresh in a UK winter comes down to three things: a clean machine, the right amount of washing powder or liquid, and getting each load dry quickly in a room with some airflow. Damp clothes that sit about for hours are what cause that musty smell, not the cold itself.

Quick answer: Take washing out as soon as the cycle ends, spin it well, and dry it on an airer in a ventilated room rather than on radiators. Dose detergent by the pack and your water hardness, and run a hot maintenance wash once a month so the machine itself doesn't start to smell.

Key Takeaways

  • Speed matters most: wet washing left in the drum is the main cause of musty laundry.

  • Airflow beats heat: an airer in a ventilated room dries better than clothes draped over radiators.

  • Less can be more: too much detergent leaves residue that holds on to smells.

  • Clean the machine: the door seal, drawer and filter need attention every few weeks.

Why Washing Smells Musty in Winter

In summer most of us can hang a load outside and have it dry by teatime. From October onwards that gets harder, so washing tends to dry indoors, slowly, in rooms with the windows shut. Fabric that stays damp for a long time gives bacteria the chance to grow, and that is where the stale, sour smell comes from.

All that water has to go somewhere, too. A full load of wet washing drying in a closed room adds moisture to the air, which can settle as condensation on cold windows and walls. Get the Wash Itself Right

Don't overload the drum

Clothes need room to move so water and detergent can reach every part of the fabric. A good rule of thumb is to leave about a hand's width of space at the top of the drum. An overloaded machine rinses badly, and clothes come out creased, damp in the middle and slower to dry.

Use the right amount of detergent

More detergent does not mean cleaner clothes. Too much leaves a film on fabric and inside the machine, and that residue traps smells. Follow the dosing guide on the pack, and use a little more only if you live in a hard water area or the load is heavily soiled.

Powder or liquid?

Both work well when used properly. Powders are a popular choice for whites, towels and everyday loads, while many people prefer liquids for darks and quick, cooler washes. Whichever you use, the care label on each garment tells you the highest temperature it can safely take.

Pick the right temperature

Cooler washes save energy and suit most everyday clothes. Towels, bedding and tea towels benefit from a hotter wash now and then, as long as the care label allows it. A hot wash once in a while also helps keep the machine fresh.

Drying Indoors Without the Damp

  • Take it out straight away. Set a timer on your phone so the load doesn't sit in the drum for hours.

  • Spin it well. A higher spin speed removes more water before drying starts, if the fabric can take it.

  • Use an airer, not radiators. Clothes on radiators block heat from the room and push moisture straight into the air.

  • Give it space. Hang items with gaps between them and shake them out first so air can move through.

  • Ventilate. Dry in one room with the door closed and a window open a crack, or use a dehumidifier or extractor fan.

Heavier items such as jumpers, jeans and towels take longest, so turn them over halfway through. Thick fabrics on sofas and cushions need a different approach altogether, which we cover in our guide on how to clean upholstery at home.

Keep the Washing Machine Fresh

A smelly machine makes smelly washing, however carefully you dry it. A few minutes every few weeks keeps it in good shape:

  1. Wipe the door seal. Pull back the rubber and clear out fluff, hair and any black marks.

  2. Clean the detergent drawer. Slide it out and rinse away any built-up residue.

  3. Check the filter. Your manual shows where it sits. Have a towel ready, as some water will come out.

  4. Run a maintenance wash. An empty hot cycle once a month clears grease and residue from the drum.

  5. Leave the door ajar. Let the drum air dry between washes.

Making Clean Washing Smell Good

A fresh scent should sit on clothes that are already properly clean. Perfume can't cover up washing that dried slowly, so get the basics above right first.

After that, scent is a personal choice. Some people like a scented washing powder that leaves a light fragrance on everything, others prefer unscented products with a fabric or room fragrance used separately. If anyone in the house has sensitive skin, test a new product on a small load first. If you are looking for luxury washing powder, Distinctive Wash (distinctivewash.co.uk) is one UK business to look at.

Store clothes only once they are completely dry. Folding slightly damp items into a drawer or wardrobe is one of the quickest ways to bring the musty smell back.

Bedding and Towels

Towels and bedding hold the most water and take the longest to dry, so they cause the most trouble in winter. Wash them on a separate day from your everyday clothes, so each load has enough airer space. Spread duvet covers over two rails of the airer rather than folding them double.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my washing smell even after a wash?

The usual causes are washing left too long in the drum, drying too slowly in a closed room, too much detergent, or a dirty machine. Fix those four and the smell usually goes.

Is it bad to dry clothes on radiators?

It is best avoided. It blocks heat from reaching the room and adds moisture to the air, which can lead to condensation. An airer in a ventilated room is the better option.

How often should I clean my washing machine?

Leave the door ajar after every wash, wipe the door seal and clean the drawer every few weeks, and run an empty hot maintenance wash about once a month.

Does a robot vacuum help in winter too?

Yes. It keeps on top of fluff, hair and the grit that gets tracked in from wet paths, which leaves more time for the laundry. See how it compares in our article on robot vacuums vs traditional cleaning.

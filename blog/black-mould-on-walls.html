Black Mould on Walls

10 November 2026

Black mould on walls can be cleaned off in an afternoon, but it nearly always comes back unless you deal with the moisture feeding it. Cleaning treats the symptom. The cause is usually condensation, a leak or damp coming through the structure, and each one needs a different fix.

Quick answer: Clean small patches of black mould with a mould remover or mild detergent, wearing gloves and a mask, then dry the area. If it returns within weeks, covers a large area or sits beside a stain, tide mark or crumbling plaster, the moisture is coming from somewhere else and needs finding.

Key Takeaways

  • Mould needs moisture, so cleaning alone rarely stops it.

  • Condensation is the most common cause in UK homes, especially in winter.

  • Patches that return fast or sit beside stains can point to a leak or penetrating damp.

  • Government health guidance warns that damp and mould can harm your health, so act early.

  • If you are buying a home with mould, find the cause before you exchange.

Why Black Mould Keeps Coming Back

Mould spores are everywhere in the air. They only grow when a surface stays damp for long enough, which is why the same cold corner of a bedroom goes black every winter. Wipe it away and the spores settle again as soon as the wall gets wet.

In most UK homes the water comes from daily life. Cooking, showers, drying clothes indoors and even breathing put moisture into the air. When that warm, damp air meets a cold wall, window reveal or the back of a wardrobe, it turns to water and the mould follows.

Condensation, Leaks or Damp in the Walls?

The pattern of the mould tells you a lot. Condensation mould tends to appear in cold spots: corners, behind furniture, around windows and on outside walls. It often comes with water running down the glass in the morning.

Mould that sits in one patch beside a brown stain or tide mark is different. That points to water getting in, perhaps from a gutter, a cracked render, a leaking pipe or a failed seal around a bath. Damp low on a ground floor wall can suggest rising or bridging damp, where moisture gets past the damp proof course. Our guide to damp and mould in UK homes covers each type in more detail.

How to Clean Black Mould Safely

For a small patch, cleaning is fine and worth doing straight away. Government guidance on damp and mould says it mainly affects the airways and lungs, and that anyone removing it should wear a mask, gloves and goggles, so protect yourself and keep the room aired while you work.

  • Wear rubber gloves, goggles and a mask that covers your nose and mouth.

  • Open windows and shut the door to the rest of the house.

  • Use a mould remover made for the surface, or warm water with a little washing-up liquid.

  • Wipe gently with a cloth rather than brushing dry spores into the air.

  • Dry the wall fully afterwards and bag the cloths for the bin.

Soft furnishings and clothes with mould on them should be washed or cleaned professionally. A full deep clean of the home can help after a long damp winter, but it only lasts if the moisture problem is fixed too.

Stopping Condensation at Home

Most condensation mould can be cut right back with a few daily habits. Open a window or run the extractor when you cook or shower. Keep lids on pans. Dry washing outside or in a ventilated room with the door closed; our tips on drying washing in winter help here. Leave a small gap between furniture and outside walls so air can move.

Steady, low heating helps more than short blasts, because walls stay warmer and less water settles on them. Check that trickle vents on windows are open and that extractor fans actually pull air. If you are planning work, a kitchen renovation is a good moment to fit an extractor that vents outside rather than recirculating.

When Cleaning Is Not Enough

Some signs mean the mould is a symptom of a building problem rather than a lifestyle one. Take a closer look, or get help, if you notice any of these:

  • Mould returns within a few weeks of cleaning, even with good ventilation.

  • The mould covers a large area. Government guidance says larger areas should be left to professionals.

  • The patch has a stain, tide mark or bubbling paint around it.

  • Plaster feels soft, crumbles or sounds hollow when tapped.

  • There is a musty smell in a room you cannot trace.

  • Skirting boards or floorboards near the patch are swollen or decaying.

In a rented home, tell your landlord in writing. Landlords in England must keep homes free from serious hazards, and damp and mould is one of the hazards councils assess. Your local council's private housing team can inspect if the landlord does not act.

Buying a Home With Mould

Fresh paint in one room of a house for sale can hide mould for a few weeks. If you see patches on a viewing, or the seller mentions damp, ask how long it has been there and what has been done. A standard mortgage valuation is not a survey and will not tell you why a wall is wet.

RICS describes home surveys in three levels. A Level 2 survey suits conventional homes in reasonable condition, while a Level 3 survey looks in more depth at older or altered properties and at defects you already suspect. If mould keeps returning in a home you are about to buy in London or Essex, a Level 3 building survey from Gibsons Surveyors can look for the cause before you commit.

Getting the Cause Fixed

Once you know the source, the fix usually follows. Condensation needs better ventilation and heating. A leak needs a roofer, plumber or reliable builder to repair the gutter, pipe or seal. Penetrating damp may need repointing, render repairs or clearing soil away from an outside wall. Treat the cause first, let the wall dry out, then clean and redecorate with a mould-resistant paint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is black mould on walls dangerous?

Government guidance from the Department of Health and Social Care and the UK Health Security Agency says damp and mould mainly affect the airways and lungs, and can also affect the eyes and skin. They can bring on or worsen allergies and asthma and raise the risk of chest infections. Children, older people, pregnant women and anyone with a health condition or weakened immune system are more at risk, so it is worth dealing with quickly.

Can I paint over black mould?

Painting over mould hides it for a short time but does not stop it. Clean and dry the wall first, fix the moisture source, then use a mould-resistant paint if you want extra protection. If you would like some help with this, get in touch with our team.

Does bleach kill black mould?

Bleach can lighten mould on hard surfaces but does not reach into porous plaster well. Government guidance on damp and mould advises using a mould and mildew product in preference to bleach, used as the label says. Never mix bleach with other cleaners.

Why does mould grow behind wardrobes?

Furniture against an outside wall stops warm air reaching it, so the wall stays cold and condensation forms. Leaving a gap of a few centimetres and keeping the room ventilated usually helps.
